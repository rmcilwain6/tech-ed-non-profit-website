Base layout · round 2

Map layout studies

Three ways to build the site around one map of British Columbia. Each one changes how the site is put together, not just how it looks. Click through the pages, try phone width, and scroll: the map responds.

Colours and type are still stand-ins, leaning warmer and rounder. The map is real: BC Albers projection, simplified from open data, drawn into the page with no map service behind it.

What carried over from round 1
  • The page receipt is on every page, now with map data sources.
  • "Next stops" end every page.
  • Bold type and a big Valleyview spotlight on Home.
  • Pillars one at a time (Story Map, Network).
  • The coast-to-Rockies cross-section, now from real elevation data.
  • Every map shows the whole province. Kamloops, Victoria and Vancouver are marked as starting regions, never as the boundary.
About the map data
  • Coastline, neighbours, highways, ferries, rivers, lakes and towns: Natural Earth (public domain).
  • 59 school district boundaries and standard K–12 schools: DataBC, Open Government Licence – British Columbia.
  • Cross-section: Copernicus DEM elevations via Open-Meteo, sampled every 1.5 km from Victoria to Kicking Horse Pass.
  • First Nations schools are a placeholder layer. Plotting them should come out of guidance from and partnership with First Nations education partners (R6), not out of a dataset.
  • Board pins are examples. Real ones appear only once members agree.
